## 1. What is compared

- **Definition.** Landesfeind & Gasteiger 2019, Eq. 5: TDF = 1 + d ln f± / d ln c, f± the mean
  **molar** activity coefficient, at constant T, p and solvent ratio. Gee 2011 calls the same
  derivative a_cc (Eq. 10), Cheng 2022 γ⁰ (text under Eq. 19).
- **Factor 2.** Lehnert 2025 (text under Eq. 6 and before Fig. 3) defines the TDF with the chemical
  potential of the neutral salt, so their TDF is **twice** Newman's, and they multiply Landesfeind's
  data by 2 to compare. We report Newman's (→ 1 at infinite dilution) and name it in the key.
- **Frame.** The experiments give a = TDF · (1 − t₊) (Landesfeind Eq. 9; Lehnert Eq. 14). An MD TDF
  from fluctuations contains no t₊, so it can be compared with a TDF only through the t₊ the paper
  used. **The like-for-like test is a(c)**, with our TDF and our t₊^SOL (ADR 0013). Lehnert also
  gives a TDF per frame (Eq. 9); its relation to Newman's TDF is not worked out here (open).

## 2. Routes

| route | source read | needs | main problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| RDF integrals (Kirkwood–Buff, KB) | Gee 2011 Eqs. 8–10 (G_ij cut at 1.2–1.5 nm, boxes > 4 nm) | RDFs | the tail of a closed box; cut-off choice |
| RDF integrals with finite-volume weight | Jones et al., OSTI 1361651, Eqs. 6–7 | RDFs, large box | scatter: "on the order of 20 %" per box-in-box value |
| **S⁰: structure factors S(k), k → 0** | **Cheng 2022 Eqs. 13–19; Wang et al. 2026 (Part II) Eqs. 20–24** | positions | slow long-wavelength relaxation (§6) |
| Free energy (insertion, integration) | not read; cited in Cheng 2022 | many runs | dense liquid, large ions; costly |
| Association model on MD shells | Phelan et al. 2025 (preprint) | RDFs | lattice-fluid model in between |

**Recommended: S⁰.** It needs only positions, has no cut-off radius, and Wang et al. give the
charge-aware small-k form (bare-Coulomb matrix, their Eq. 20) that ordinary Ornstein–Zernike fits
lack. KB integrals as a cross-check on the same trajectories.

## 3. Equations

Species α ∈ {ions, EC, EMC}. Cation and anion together are one species with density c₁ = 2c_e
(indistinguishable ions: Gee 2011; "a valid route for this binary electrolyte": Wang et al. §III A).
The fluctuation matrix B_αβ = ⟨δN_α δN_β⟩/V at k → 0 is B_αβ = √(c_α c_β) S⁰_αβ (Cheng Eq. 14). With
A = B⁻¹, u = A c, w = c·u (**mine**, the standard KB inversion for any number of components):

> TDF_m = c₁ (A₁₁ − u₁²/w),  V̄_α = u_α/w,  TDF = TDF_m / (1 − c₁ V̄₁)          (1)

TDF_m is the derivative by molality (solvent amounts fixed), TDF by molarity. Checked numerically
(`A1.7/tdf_check.py`): for one solvent (1) equals Gee Eq. 10 to round-off; Σ c_α V̄_α = 1.

**EC and EMC must stay separate.** Lumping them into one solvent replaces "solvent ratio fixed"
by "solvent ratio free to relax around the ions", another derivative, and it is biased low as soon
as the ions prefer either solvent. On a toy matrix (ideal mixture plus an ion–EC term, mine; same
script):

| ion–EC term A₁₂ (L/mol) | TDF, three species (1) | TDF, EC + EMC lumped |
|---|---|---|
| −0.20 (ions attract EC) | 1.027 | 0.847 |
| −0.10 | 0.995 | 0.954 |
| 0 | 0.963 | 0.964 |
| +0.10 | 0.930 | 0.880 |
| +0.20 (ions avoid EC) | 0.896 | 0.708 |

Our models deplete EC in the Li⁺ shell, experiment and QM enrich it (trials T2, T6), so neither
the real nor the model liquid has the neutral case, and this is not a detail. (t₊ in the SOL
frame lumps the *velocity* of EC and EMC, which is a convention; here the lumping would be an
error.)

By-products: V̄ of the salt (ν_e) from the same matrix, a check of the density fit used by ADR 0013;
and the isothermal compressibility.

## 4. Published values to reproduce first (rule 4)

1. **Binary LJ mixture, Cheng 2022, Fig. 2a (numbers printed in the figure):** purely repulsive
   6–12 LJ, cut and shifted at 2^{1/6}σ, ε_AA = 1.2, ε_BB = 1.0, ε_AB = 1.1, T* = 1.2, P* = 2,
   x_A = 0.5: **S⁰_AB = −0.439 ± 0.004 (N = 4000), −0.436 ± 0.005 (N = 23 328)**. 10⁷ steps at
   Δt* = 10⁻³ for N = 4000. CPU-sized. ε_AB is the arithmetic mean, so it needs a spec 0.1.2 pair
   override; the cut-and-shifted potential has to be checked against the engine's LJ options.
2. **NaCl in water, JC/SPC/E:** excess chemical potential against c from four independent methods
   (Wang et al. Fig. 2; Cheng 2022 Fig. 4). Read off the figures, so the target is the shape (the
   minimum and the slope between 1 and 4 M), with the reading error stated. Rigid SPC/E needs the
   constraints of E2.5. Their size: 32 640 water molecules, 4 ns.
3. **Carbonate electrolyte: no published MD value found** for LiPF₆ in carbonates. Lehnert 2025
   states that the direct route exists (thermodynamic integration, KB, S⁰) but was "not done in
   this work". Jones et al. give KB factors for LiBF₄ in PC/EC as figures with about 20 % errors;
   their definition (∂μ/∂c) differs. So our value for EC:EMC would be new, and is reported only
   after 1 and 2 pass.

Proposed criterion: target 1 within 2 combined standard errors of the N = 4000 value; target 2
within the reading error and 2 SE. The author may prefer other limits; they are fixed before the
runs.

## 5. Functions and tests

- `structure_factor.py`: `partial_sk(traj, species, k_max)` on scaled coordinates (Cheng Eq. 17) from
  molecular centres of mass and single ions, binned in |k|; `s0(sk, form="oz" | "bc")`, the
  Ornstein–Zernike fit (Cheng Eq. 18) and the bare-Coulomb matrix fit (Wang Eq. 20), with the
  fit range as an explicit input and a scan over it in the output.
- `thermodynamic_factor.py`: equation (1), block errors, `TDF_newman` as the key name.
- CLI: `ucmd-analyze tdf TRAJ.h5 --temperature-K T --cation Li --anion PF6`.

Tests, all with known answers:
1. Ideal gas mixture: S_αβ = δ_αβ within errors, TDF = 1.
2. Algebra: (1) against Gee Eq. 10 (one solvent) to 1e-12; Σ c_α V̄_α = 1; three identical species
   labelled differently give the one-species result.
3. A Gaussian field with a prescribed S(k) matrix on the reciprocal lattice: S⁰ and the TDF are
   recovered within the block errors, with both fit forms.
4. Charge neutrality: the charge structure factor of a neutral synthetic system falls as k²
   (Wang Eq. 26); the fit must not be applied below the smallest k in the box.
5. Too short a run is refused: block length against the fitted relaxation time of the smallest k.

## 6. Cost (mine)

A concentration wave relaxes with τ = 1/(D k²). With 6 independent real modes at the smallest k of
a cubic box the relative error of S there is √(2/(6 T/τ)). Throughput from the pilot hardware:
about 90 ns/day for 7036 atoms (shared GPU), scaled with 1/N.

| box L (nm) | atoms | k_min (nm⁻¹) | τ (ns) at D = 1.0 / 2.5 · 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s | error of S(k_min) after 100 ns | 100 ns on the GPU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.4 | 7 036 | 1.43 | 4.9 / 2.0 | 13 % / 8 % | ≈ 1.1 days |
| 6.0 | 18 000 | 1.05 | 9.1 / 3.6 | 17 % / 11 % | ≈ 3 days |
| 8.0 | 42 000 | 0.79 | 16 / 6.5 | 23 % / 15 % | ≈ 7 days |

- The next k-shells add modes and relax faster, so the fitted S⁰ is better than these numbers, but
  **a TDF to about ±5 % needs some 200–300 ns per concentration**; at the pilot's D (too slow) more.
- **Concentrations:** 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 M for the trend; at least two box sizes at 1 M.
- **Existing runs first:** the 20 ns pilot is long enough for a run of the code (τ ≈ 5 ns), not for
  a value (error ≈ 30 %).
- The pilot is NVT, Cheng's runs are NPT. Cheng states that the two are equivalent in the
  thermodynamic limit (text under Eq. 17); for our box this is an assumption, tested on target 1 by
  running both.
