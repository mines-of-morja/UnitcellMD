Summary

  • Aim. Predict the transport properties of LiPF₆ in carbonate solvents (κ, t₊, D) from molecular dynamics and test them against the measurements of Landesfeind & Gasteiger [1]. Transport data are kept as test data: no model parameter is fitted to them.
  • The software is verified independently of any force field. Energies agree with OpenMM to ${q_m2a_energy} on a 7036-atom electrolyte, densities under pressure control within ${q_argon_npt_sigma} standard error, and the GPU backend must reproduce the CPU reference before every run.
  • First result: κ is a factor ${kappa_ratio} too low. With open OPLS-AA parameters and Lehnert's set-up [2], 1 M LiPF₆ in EC:EMC (3:7 w:w) at 20 °C gives κ = ${kappa} S/m, against ${kappa_exp} S/m from Landesfeind's fit. Even the uncorrelated Nernst–Einstein value is too low, so the ions move too slowly; association does not explain it.
  • The Li⁺ shell holds too little EC. Our model has ${pi_ec} EC per Li⁺ within 0.516 nm, Lehnert's ${le_ec}. Dielectric spectroscopy [3] gives at most about ${drs_ec} bound EC per Li⁺. Gas-phase quantum chemistry shows the cause: Ebind(EC) − Ebind(DMC) is ${dqm} kcal/mol in QM, but ${dm0} in the open force field.
  • Next: solvent parameters chosen from molecular and pure-liquid data, the ion-charge scaling taken from the refractive index rather than fitted to κ, and pass criteria for κ fixed before the production runs.

1. Approach

The model is built and tested in layers: single molecules against quantum chemistry, pure liquids against density and heat of vaporization, one ion in solvent against binding and solvation data, and only then the electrolyte. Three rules hold throughout.

  • Fit data and test data are fixed in advance. Transport (κ, t₊, D, residence times) is never fitted. The proposed pass criteria for the first predictive model are κ(1 M, 20 °C) within ±15 % of the measured value without refit, and the concentration of maximum κ within ±0.2 M.
  • A method reproduces a published number before it produces a new one. This applies to the engine, the force field and the quantum chemistry (section 2).
  • Errors stay on record. Section 5 lists the defects found so far and how each was caught.

UnitcellMD has three parts: an engine with a bitwise-reproducible CPU reference backend (Rust) and an OpenMM GPU backend, a system builder, and an analysis library for structure and Onsager transport. All are open source (Apache-2.0).

3. Baseline model: densities and transport

The baseline follows Lehnert et al. [2] with openly available parameters: OPLS-AA conventions, EC and EMC from LigParGen (“M0”), Li⁺ from Jensen & Jorgensen, PF₆⁻ from CL&P, and ion charges scaled by ζ = 0.75 (Lehnert's value). Lehnert's box has 47 ion pairs at 1.0 M.

Reading. Lehnert's model matches κ at 1 M by construction, because ζ is fitted there. Ours is not fitted and comes out a factor ${kappa_ratio} low. The Nernst–Einstein κ, which ignores all ion–ion correlations, is still a factor ${kne_ratio} low. So the ions are too slow; association does not explain the deficit. Likely causes: a too viscous solvent (EMC is too dense) and a Li⁺ that drags an EMC-only shell. Viscosity is being checked; the shell is section 4. Reaching κ to 10 % needs about ${q_noise_kappa} per state point, t₊ to 10 % about ${q_noise_tplus}.

4. The Li⁺ solvation shell

  • Our baseline excludes EC. At 1 M, ${pi_ec} EC and ${pi_emc} EMC carbonyl O sit within 0.516 nm, plus ${pi_pf6} PF₆⁻. So EC holds ${pi_share} % of the carbonyl O at Li⁺, while it is ${ec_mol_pct} % of the solvent molecules. Lehnert's model, with the same ζ, gives ${le_ec} EC and ${le_emc} EMC (${le_share} % EC).
  • Experimental anchor. Self, Hahn and Persson [3] measured dielectric spectra of EC:EMC (3:7 w:w) and of 1 M LiPF₆ in it at 21 °C. The free-EC amplitude drops from ${drs_a_neat} to ${drs_a_1m}. If the amplitude is proportional to the free-EC concentration, ${drs_frac} % of the EC (${drs_cec} mol/L) is bound, about ${drs_ec} ± ${drs_ec_err} EC per Li⁺. Two effects make this an upper bound: the dilution of EC by the salt, and kinetic depolarization. This is our reading of their data, not their conclusion.
  • Gas-phase binding shows the cause (Fig. 4). The difference Ebind(EC) − Ebind(DMC) is ${dqm} kcal/mol in quantum chemistry, ${dm0} in the baseline force field and ${dm1} with the published OPLS-AA EC charges (“M1”). ${qm_emc_sentence} All fixed-charge models under-bind Li⁺ by ${under_lo}–${under_hi} kcal/mol in vacuum against G4 [5] (missing induction). That part is common to all solvents; the difference between solvents decides the preference.
  • Liquid test of M1 (Fig. 3b). Over ${n_M0} M0 and ${n_M1} M1 runs, the EC share of the first shell is ${M0_first} % and ${M1_first} %; within 0.516 nm it is ${M0_wide} % and ${M1_wide} %. Correcting the charges moves the shell towards EC, but by itself not to the experimental level.

6. Next steps

  • Replicas of the shell test (Fig. 3b, in progress) and M1 at 1 M against the DRS estimate.
  • M2: solvent charges from quantum chemistry (RESP2), tested against densities, heats of vaporization and the gas-phase binding differences. M3: ζ from the refractive index (electronic continuum correction), not fitted to κ. Explicit polarization only if M3 fails in a specific, measured way.
  • Then the transport campaign for the model that passes: κ(c, T), t₊, D and the thermodynamic factor for 1 M EC:EMC over concentration and temperature, at about ${q_noise_kappa} per state point. Decompositions that only MD provides: ion correlations behind the κ maximum, viscosity against association in κ(T), and cluster contributions to t₊.
  • Publication: a software paper (UnitcellMD with this electrolyte as the validated application), planned for J. Chem. Inf. Model., not before May 2027.
