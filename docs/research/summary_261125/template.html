Summary

  • Aim, unchanged. Predict κ, t₊ and D of LiPF₆ in EC:EMC from molecular dynamics and test them against Landesfeind & Gasteiger [1]. No parameter is fitted to transport data. No predictive model exists yet, so the κ criteria have not been applied.
  • Which parameters slow the ions? ${prelim_word} scan at 1 M, 20 °C (section 1): the ion charge scale ζ and the EMC attraction ε change the Nernst–Einstein κ (κNE) far more than the size of Li⁺, whose effect is not resolved. This is a map, not a fit.
  • The Li⁺ clusters converged (trial T6, section 2). The energies that would turn them into a correction are on hold: by the rule fixed beforehand, they run only if the Li⁺ size moves transport. The author decides.
  • The water pressure puzzle has a cause: the 1 fs step with flexible bonds (section 3). The liquid is warmer than displayed, by ${q_t_el} K in the electrolyte.
  • Densities were re-checked (section 4). DMC fails ±2 %, which the 23 November summary had counted as passing.

Reading rule: a knob moves transport if ln κNE changes by at least 0.2 over the range and by at least 2 SE. ${rule_line} Run-to-run noise of κNE: ${noise_k} % per run (SD, pooled from the baselines and T2).

  • ζ and ε(EMC) move transport. Smaller charges and weaker EMC attraction speed up the ions. ε(EMC) changes D of Li⁺ and EMC by about the same exponent, which is what a change of the solvent's viscosity would do. The scan measures no viscosity, so trial T4 (viscosity or Li⁺ binding) is not decided by it.
  • σ(Li⁺) is not resolved. ln κNE changes by ${s_sigmaLi_change} ± ${s_sigmaLi_se} over the range; with 2 SE the data allow ${s_sigmaLi_lo2} to ${s_sigmaLi_hi2}. That is not proof of no effect. The knob did change the shell: EC per Li⁺ went from ${sig_ec_lo} to ${sig_ec_hi}. More EC in the shell did not visibly speed up or slow down the ions in this range.
  • ε(EMC) also moves the density, down to ${emc_dens_lo} g/cm³ at the lowest probe, against ${q_m1_rho} for M1 and ${rho_lf} in the fit of [1]. The direction agrees with EMC being too dense on its own (section 4).
  • Noise is larger than expected. The baselines of this scan give ${k_base_lo} and ${k_base_hi} S/m, the T2 runs ${t2_lo}–${t2_hi} S/m. One probe alone says little; the trends rest on several probes.
  • No parameter is chosen here. Transport is never used to choose one (trials rule 2).

3. The 1 fs time step and flexible bonds

At Wu's density, SPC/Fw water gave ${q_p_gpu} bar on the GPU and ${q_p_cpu} on the CPU, when about 1 bar was expected. Re-evaluating the stored frames (no new simulation) gives the cause:

  • Two definitions differ by ${q_p_bias}: the CPU engine reports the atomic pressure, the GPU the molecular one. With the same definition the engines agree: about ${q_p_cpu_mol} (CPU, 298.16 K) against ${q_p_gpu}. On the GPU, the NPT density is the 1 atm density of the NVT (P = ${q_p_at_npt} at that density). Whether the CPU barostat's density is biased is not shown.
  • The liquid is warmer than displayed, by ${q_t_water} K in water (runs ${q_t_water_range}) and ${q_t_el} K in the electrolyte. The thermostat holds the on-step kinetic energy of all atoms, and the fast bonds show too little of it. So D and κ come out a little too high and the density a little too low; the deficit of κ against experiment (M0: a factor ${kappa_ratio}) goes the other way and is not explained.
  • Open. At 0.5 fs the excess is ${q_t_half} K where ${q_t_half_exp} K is expected (${q_sigma17}). Of the ${q_p_gpu} bar, ${q_p_temp} come from the temperature, and ${q_p_rest} bar remain unexplained.
